# Offer brief: ten persuasion lenses

Use this reference in `offer` mode, or before outlining when the offer is unclear. The output is a working brief tied to the buyer's evidence and the business's actual terms. It supplies decisions for a sales page; it does not automatically become publishable copy.

## Course-derived framework

Work through these ten lenses in order. For each, record the proposed answer, its supporting source, and any unresolved question. Separate what the business can establish from what research still needs to test.

1. **Problem.** Identify the intended buyer and the specific problem the offer addresses. Prefer a recognizable situation over a broad demographic or a claim that everyone needs the product. State what success would change for this person.
2. **Pain.** Describe how the problem affects the buyer's daily experience: obstacles, frustrations, uncertainty, and costs they recognize. Use actual customer language when available. First-person brainstorms can suggest research questions, but cannot stand in for customer testimony.
3. **Prescription.** Explain the general category of solution before naming the branded product. Establish why this kind of approach could address the problem and what conditions it requires. Keep category evidence distinct from evidence that the specific product works.
4. **Pivot.** Connect the creator or business to the solution through a relevant reason for building it and credible qualifications. Include only the history a buyer needs to understand the offer. A personal story should help explain the product's purpose or authority.
5. **Positioning.** Identify the alternatives buyers use, where those alternatives create friction, and the offer's supported differences. Examine whether it is easier, faster, or better for this audience. Do not assert those comparisons without defining the comparison and its evidence.
6. **Product.** Name the product and articulate its distinctive promise or mechanism. Tie the positioning to a concrete reason to choose it. If the proposed distinction is common among competitors, mark it as an unresolved positioning hypothesis.
7. **Product Features.** Inventory what is actually included, including genuine bonuses. Translate each feature into its functional benefit: what it enables the buyer to do. Keep this separate from the emotional experience explored later in the page.
8. **Price.** Record the actual regular price and any relevant, researched alternative cost or value comparison. Specify what the comparison includes and where it differs. A buyer should be able to understand why the comparison is fair.
9. **Promotion.** Document any real promotional price, limited quantity, bonus availability, or offer window and the reason for it. Capture the exact terms, dates, timezone, and eligibility when relevant. If there is no promotion, record that plainly.
10. **Purchase Details.** Explain how to buy and what happens next: payment methods or plans, delivery, access timing, onboarding, and support. Gather recurring purchase questions. Flag unknown checkout or fulfillment details for the owner to resolve.

## Practical plugin adaptations and safeguards

Use a compact table: `Lens | Working answer | Evidence/source | Status or gap`. Label each answer **provided fact**, **verified evidence**, **hypothesis**, **missing**, or **N/A**, with a reason for N/A. A user-provided fact is usable input but is not automatically independently verified. Link the specific document, customer research, policy, product page, or approved business statement that supports material claims. Never turn a hypothesis into a fact just because it sounds persuasive.

AI can generate possible pains, objections, alternatives, and differentiators for investigation. Label that output as brainstorming. Do not invent customer quotations, testimonials, success rates, qualifications, or product capabilities to fill gaps.

The course's price and promotion lenses are prompts to investigate the offer, not requirements to create a discount. Do not manufacture a regular price, inflate bonus values, sum unsupported savings, or invent scarcity. An evergreen offer can omit promotional mechanics and explain its actual price directly.

Finish with the decisions that are ready, the few gaps that materially affect the page, and the next recommended mode. Carry unresolved claims into an evidence checklist or use explicit drafting placeholders. A complete brief is not proof that checkout, delivery, or the offer itself has been tested.
