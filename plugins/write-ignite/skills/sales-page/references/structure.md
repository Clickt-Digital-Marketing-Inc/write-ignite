# Sales-page structure

Use this reference for `outline` and `draft` modes. Start from the offer brief and buyer evidence. Preserve the progression from a recognized problem, to the category of solution, to the branded offer and purchase decision. These are structural functions: combine adjacent sections when useful, and mark inapplicable commercial mechanics N/A with a reason.

## Course-derived sequence

1. **Headline.** Establish relevance immediately. An optional pre-headline identifies the audience; the main headline conveys a supported benefit, problem, or mechanism; a sub-headline can develop the invitation or next action.
2. **Lead-In.** Show an accurate understanding of the buyer's problem and experience. Make the stakes recognizable without exaggerating or intensifying distress.
3. **Discovery.** Introduce the solution category and the reasoning or evidence behind it before the branded product. Explain why this approach fits the problem. Category evidence does not automatically prove product performance.
4. **Pivot.** Briefly connect the creator or business to the solution through relevant purpose, experience, or qualifications.
5. **USP.** Explain the useful difference from alternatives, then introduce the branded product. Keep comparisons specific and supported.
6. **Offer.** Show what the buyer receives. Pair each included feature with a functional benefit: what it helps them accomplish.
7. **Emotional benefits.** Develop approximately five to eight plausible emotional benefit scenarios where the evidence and offer support them. Describe what the functional change could feel like in everyday life. Treat these as possibilities, not guaranteed personal outcomes.
8. **Value.** Explain a fair, researched comparison with the cost or effort of relevant alternatives. Show the basis and limits of any numerical comparison.
9. **Regular Price.** State the actual regular price when one exists and is relevant. If there is only one current price, do not fabricate an anchor.
10. **Promotion.** Explain a real current offer or discount and its terms. Omit this function when no promotion exists.
11. **Bonus Stacking.** Present genuine extras and why each matters. Do not assign unsupported standalone prices or relabel necessary core features as artificial bonuses.
12. **Scarcity.** State only actual limits on time, quantity, bonuses, or offer availability. Keep the scope of each limit clear.
13. **Reason Why.** Explain the real reason behind an applicable limit or promotion. Without a verified reason, flag the gap instead of inventing a story.
14. **Purchase Details.** Give the buyer a clear action and describe the immediate next steps, including delivery, access, or onboarding.
15. **Risk Reversal.** Explain actual refund or guarantee policies, payment options, and relevant security information. Preserve conditions and exclusions. Do not invent a guarantee or security certification.
16. **CTA.** Present the purchase action, accurate price, relevant options, and button wording. Avoid uncertainty about what clicking or buying does.

## Optional elements and CTA locations

A fuller bio can follow the Pivot when the person's identity matters to the offer. Relevant social proof can follow the Offer, emotional benefits, Reason Why, or CTA. A complex offer may need a concise recap immediately before the CTA. FAQs can follow the CTA; repeat a final CTA after lower-page proof or FAQs so readers can act where they finish.

The reviewed checklist recommends CTA opportunities above the fold; after the lead-in and prescription; after the offer breakdown; after value and bonuses; after purchase details; and at the bottom. Map these to the page's actual sections rather than mechanically duplicating a button after every heading. In a text draft, use explicit annotations such as `[CTA: View plans → pricing section]`. State the intended action and destination.

## Practical plugin adaptations and safeguards

For an outline, provide `Section/function | Buyer question | Message | Evidence | CTA or asset need`. For a draft, write usable copy and keep evidence gaps visibly separate from polished claims. Link sources for material claims and proof; do not invent testimonials, comparative results, deadlines, prices, or valuations.

Keep functional benefits and emotional scenarios distinct even if both appear in one section. Preserve N/A explanations in the working notes so omitted promotions or guarantees are deliberate. Use the user's brand voice. Copy annotations are not implementation evidence: above-fold visibility, link destinations, mobile layout, and checkout behavior need separate runtime checks.
