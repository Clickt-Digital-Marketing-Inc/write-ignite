# Anti-slop foundation

This foundation applies before planning or writing and during final review. It uses Peter Yang's [no-ai-slop](https://github.com/petergyang/no-ai-slop), pinned at commit `000650b156983f5159695b441477f4e63b25dc85`, retrieved 2026-09-27. The source files and [MIT license](no-ai-slop/LICENSE) are bundled so the workflow does not depend on another installed skill or live repository access.

Read [the upstream rules](no-ai-slop/rules.md) for every writing or review task, and use [the upstream evaluation](no-ai-slop/eval.md) before delivering copy. These source files are unmodified; the original `SKILL.md` is named `rules.md` here to make it a reference rather than an independently discovered skill. Its relative `eval.md` link still resolves.

## Apply the rules to sales pages

1. Identify the buyer, intended action and voice. On rewrites, notice and preserve the original vocabulary, rhythm, humor, bluntness, useful asides and honest uncertainty. Keep strong sentences.
2. While drafting, choose real product details, mechanisms, examples and consequences instead of universal praise. Use only supported details. Specificity is not permission to invent a statistic.
3. Examine the draft for the named patterns in the upstream rules. Look beyond a banned-word scan: structure, repeated cadence, generic hooks, fake drama, weak attribution and empty endings can still make familiar words read like a template.
4. Make the smallest edits that resolve those problems while preserving the purpose, facts, conditions and voice.
5. Evaluate the result, repair failed applicable checks, and read the changed passages in context. For a rewrite, return the full requested revised text with a short change summary.

When asked only to detect slop, return `pattern | exact passage | brief fix`. Do not rewrite the page, assign a score, or infer whether AI wrote it. If no listed pattern is evident, say that without certifying human authorship.

## Resolve differences with the course guidance

These are plugin adaptations to apply both sources coherently:

- **Section functions versus stock phrasing.** Keep the course's sales argument while expressing it in fresh, specific language. Avoid rhetorical question/answer hooks, binary contrast slogans, artificial dramatic reveals and generic transition fillers. A genuine logistical question in an FAQ is useful information, not automatically a rhetorical setup.
- **Clarity versus rigid shape.** The sixteen functions may merge or be marked not applicable. Do not force a repeated paragraph pattern or fifteen-word sentence limit. Keep a clear longer sentence, distinctive fragment or personal aside when it serves the reader and brand.
- **Useful formatting versus decoration.** Keep headings, inclusion lists, price labels and buttons that help people find information. Use bold only when it improves scanning; remove decorative emphasis. An offer recap can help a reader compare several components; a generic closing recap adds little. Finish with the real next action.
- **Sources versus invented specificity.** The upstream before/after examples illustrate style, not facts to reuse. Never substitute their numbers or claims into client work. Preserve exact quotes, brand/product names, legally required text and technical terms; flag concerns separately instead of corrupting them to satisfy a word ban.
- **Voice versus gimmicks.** Preserve authentic uncertainty and character without adding forced slang, profanity, mistakes, fake anecdotes or dramatic punctuation. No requirement to imitate Peter Yang or Alex Cattoni.
- **Drafting versus editing.** The upstream request to ask for a draft applies to an editing task without one. If the user asks for new copy and supplies a brief, write it. Reuse a known audience and action instead of asking again.

Explicit user choices and factual accuracy take precedence over style defaults. Course language is instructional scaffolding, not approved customer-facing copy. Apply anti-slop to the visible language while retaining the commercial information the buyer needs.

## Evidence of review

Check meaning, voice preservation, concrete details, proportional edits, named patterns, natural cadence, formatting and ending. Distinguish a text-based rhythm check from a spoken review. A successful style check supports an editorial conclusion only; it does not predict conversion or establish authorship.
